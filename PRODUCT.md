# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **Medical Representatives (MRs)** in Indian pharma, on a laptop between field calls. They learn product, doctor engagement, compliance and territory skills in short sessions, then rehearse doctor conversations with the AI Doctor.
- **Area managers** coaching 10–15 MRs. They check who needs help, review practical assignments and read practice trends.
- **Training admins** building courses, assigning learning paths, configuring the AI Doctor and reporting on compliance.
- **The client audience for this demo:** pharma training and commercial leaders who click through it themselves on their own laptops to judge whether it fits their field force.

## Product Purpose

A capability-development platform that takes an MR from "I know very little about pharmaceutical selling" to certified in product, doctor engagement, territory, compliance and global markets. Learning is structured Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced → Certification. Practice happens with a realistic but fictional AI Doctor, and coaching comes from a rubric-based AI Coach. Success means the client trusts it as a serious, compliant training tool they could roll out to their field force.

## Brand Personality

**Clinical, trustworthy, calm.** It should feel like precise medical-education software: quiet confidence, nothing flashy, everything legible. Copy is plain and factual. Encouragement is earned by results, never by hype.

## Anti-references

- Childish or gamified learning apps: cartoon badges, confetti, streak fireworks, leaderboards that turn commercial performance into competition.
- Generic SaaS templates: gradient heroes, glass cards, hero-metric dashboards, identical icon-card grids.
- Consumer health apps with soft pastel illustration styles.

## Design Principles

1. **Compliance is visible, not hidden.** Approved information, critical errors and privacy notices are first-class parts of the interface, never footnotes.
2. **Legibility before style.** Every screen should read comfortably at arm's length on a laptop; type and contrast are chosen for reading, not decoration.
3. **Earned familiarity.** Standard navigation, tables, tabs and forms behave the way users of good enterprise tools expect.
4. **Motion explains state.** Transitions show loading, arrival, change and feedback, and never perform for their own sake.
5. **Scores trace to evidence.** Every number shown to an MR or manager comes from a defined assessment or rubric.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for all text and interactive states.
- Full keyboard navigation with visible focus.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` disables non-essential animation.
- Never ask users for identifiable patient information; redact it automatically if entered.
